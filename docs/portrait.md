# Holographic portrait source

`assets/portrait/evan-face.json` is a stylized face surface derived from the owner's supplied frontal photograph, cross-checked against the brighter second photograph. Original photographs are not included in the public site.

The geometry was extracted entirely on this computer with MediaPipe Tasks Vision 0.10.3 and Google's `face_landmarker/float16/1` model, in IMAGE mode. The portrait does not load MediaPipe, a recognition service, or a camera at runtime. No photograph was uploaded for extraction. Face depth and landmarks hidden by the fringe are monocular estimates, not a volumetric scan.

The 478 positions include iris landmarks; the 1,322 undirected surface edges and named eye, brow, lip, iris, and oval connections use MediaPipe's published topology. The image pose was removed with the inverse detected rotation. X points right, Y points up, and positive Z points toward the viewer. X/Y are centered on the face oval; its width is 2 units. `bounds`, `ovalBounds`, and `landmarks` describe the geometry for the renderer.

Sources: [MediaPipe Face Landmarker guide](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker), [JavaScript API and landmark connections](https://developers.google.com/edge/api/mediapipe/js/tasks-vision.facelandmarker), [Apache 2.0 MediaPipe source](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe).

`portrait-geometry.js` creates a bald hologram with 3,087 spatial points and 7,251 fine filaments. The source face triangles are subdivided once; their irregular topology, eye sockets, nose, cheek and jaw proportions remain recognizable without becoming a photograph. A flatter derived mouth surface and two slim, interrupted traces replace the realistic lip contours. Almond-shaped optical rims, smooth iris arcs, open pupils and small highlights replace the original diamond-shaped iris loops and radial spokes. Receding skull rings close behind the face without ears, and a tapered neck dissolves into sparse triangular fragments. There are no solid armor plates or hair. The first 478 positions preserve their exact source coordinates and ordering as anchors; iris centers 468 and 473 remain stable. Hidden anchors are excluded from visible point strokes, and the original source file stays unchanged.

`portrait.js` projects the coordinates with true perspective onto a transparent Canvas 2D surface. Depth-faded filaments and small points leave darkness between the triangles; fine ice-blue and violet optical filaments provide a slightly different eye accent. Colors take their base from the site settings, then vary subtly toward cool projection light. A spring-driven yaw, pitch and roll give the head acceleration and gentle follow-through. Gaze settles independently, cursor gravity bends the nearby lattice, and small breathing, wave and fragment offsets keep the projection from feeling rigid. Thousands of edges are batched into a few dozen canvas paths by color and opacity, rather than issuing a separate stroke for every edge. No WebGL context is needed.

The host's `data-ai-state` accepts `idle`, `thinking`, `responding`, or `error`. Thinking accelerates an energy sweep through the face and temple lattice and pulses the optical filaments. A bounded generative neural field appears behind the mesh: twelve curved signal sheets, two partial tilted orbits, and forty linked particles drift at different rates. Responding moves a speech-like rhythm through the mouth while both the iris and almond rims blend through adjacent neon phosphor hues. Separate attack and decay envelopes fade the background effects and optical spectrum back to the idle palette smoothly. Dispatch `portrait:aistatechange` on the host after updating the state so even a static reduced-motion portrait refreshes immediately. `data-chat-open="true"` dims the projection and shifts its light toward blue/violet; dispatch `portrait:chatvisibilitychange` when opening or closing. `data-activity-state`, `data-point-count` and `data-edge-count` expose normalized state and geometry counts for diagnostics. Reduced motion uses still neural artwork and a fixed eye accent; it never starts an animation loop. Hidden tabs and off-screen projections pause animation. Page navigation aborts the fetch and removes observers, state-event listeners, and animation frames.

The build generates `assets/portrait/evan-projection.svg` and a matching `evan-projection-eyes.svg` from the same geometry through `scripts/portrait-fallback.mjs`. An internal luminance mask preserves the fine filament opacity in the external CSS alpha mask. The two color masks preserve the full wireframe and its separate optical accent without JavaScript or when the live renderer cannot load.

On the home page, `portrait-chat.js` opens a fixed-size glass conversation overlay over the clickable projection. Messages scroll internally, so opening chat and revealing long replies do not move the hero, career cards, Terminal, or footer. The controller sets `data-chat-open` and dispatches `portrait:chatvisibilitychange`; the underlying face dims and shifts toward blue-violet while the overlay is open. The existing Gemini endpoint supplies real replies; a local typewriter presentation reveals completed response text while the face enters its responding state. Requests and typing use separate thinking/responding states, with an immediate full reply for reduced motion. Responses are plain text, recent complete turns are bounded to the server's input limits, and failures retain a retry action. Closing cancels pending requests or completes an already-received response; page navigation aborts requests and disposes listeners and animation frames. Conversations stay in memory for that page visit. Other pages retain the existing modal chat.
